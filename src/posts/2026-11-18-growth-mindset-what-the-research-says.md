"Growth mindset" is everywhere, from classrooms to offices to Instagram quotes. But what is it, and does it actually work?

## The idea

Psychologist **Carol Dweck** described two ways of thinking about ability:

- **Fixed mindset:** "I'm just not a maths person." Ability is something you have or you don't.
- **Growth mindset:** "I'm not good at this *yet*." Ability can develop with effort, good strategies and help from others.

## What the evidence shows

This is where it gets interesting. Large studies have found that the effects of growth mindset programmes are **real but smaller than the hype suggests**, and they seem to help most for certain groups, such as students who were struggling.

That doesn't make the idea useless. It means growth mindset isn't magic: believing you can improve helps most when it comes with **effort, better strategies and support**.

## How to use it well

1. **Add "yet"** to your self-talk: "I can't do this *yet*."
2. **Change strategy, not just effort.** If something isn't working, try a different approach instead of simply trying harder.
3. **Ask for feedback.** Growth happens faster with help.

The goal isn't to believe you can do anything. It's to believe you can get better, and then act like it.
