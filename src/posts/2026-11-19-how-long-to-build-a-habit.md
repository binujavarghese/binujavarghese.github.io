You've probably heard that it takes **21 days** to form a new habit. It's a nice, neat number. Unfortunately, it isn't based on research.

## What a real study found

In a well-known study, researchers at University College London led by **Phillippa Lally** followed 96 people as they tried to build a new daily habit, such as drinking water with lunch or going for a short run.

They found:

- On average, it took about **66 days** for the behaviour to become automatic.
- But the range was huge: from **18 days to 254 days**, depending on the person and the habit.
- **Missing a single day didn't ruin progress.** What mattered was coming back.

## Why this is good news

If you've tried a habit for three weeks and it still feels hard, you haven't failed. You're simply in the middle of the process.

## What to take from it

1. **Expect it to take months, not weeks.** Be patient with yourself.
2. **Keep it simple.** Easier habits become automatic faster.
3. **Don't let one missed day become two.** Just restart tomorrow.

Habits aren't built in a magic number of days. They're built by showing up, imperfectly, again and again.
