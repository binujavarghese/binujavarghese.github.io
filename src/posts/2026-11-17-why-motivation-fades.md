We've all been there. On Monday you're full of energy: new plan, new routine, new you. By Thursday, the energy has quietly gone, and you're wondering what's wrong with you.

Nothing is wrong with you. Motivation is a **feeling**, and like all feelings, it comes and goes.

## The problem with waiting to feel motivated

If we only act when we feel motivated, we hand control of our goals to our mood. And mood depends on sleep, stress, the weather and a hundred other things we can't control.

## What works better: deciding in advance

One of the most useful ideas in motivation research is the **implementation intention**, studied by psychologist Peter Gollwitzer. It's a simple "if–then" plan:

> If it's 7 a.m. and I've made my coffee, then I'll write for 10 minutes.

Instead of a vague goal ("write more"), you link the action to a specific moment. A large review of studies by Gollwitzer and Sheeran found that people who made plans like this were noticeably more likely to follow through on their goals.

## Try this today

1. Pick **one** small action that matters to you.
2. Write it as an "if–then" plan: *If [time or situation], then I will [action].*
3. Make the action so small it feels almost too easy.

Motivation will still come and go. But with a plan in place, you won't need it as much.
